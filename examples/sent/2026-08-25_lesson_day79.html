Day 79: Streaming Responses – TTFT vs Total Latency
TL;DR
Learn how time‑to‑first‑token (TTFT) and total latency shape user experience for streamed AI outputs and how to optimize them in production.

In plain English

Streaming lets a model send tokens to the client as soon as they are generated instead of waiting for the whole answer. The moment the first token arrives is called time‑to‑first‑token (TTFT); the time from request to the final token is total latency. TTFT is a perception metric—users start reading earlier—while total latency measures the actual end‑to‑end speed of the service.
If TTFT is low but total latency is high, users may feel the system is responsive at first but become frustrated when the answer drags on. Conversely, a high TTFT can make the interface feel sluggish even if the final answer arrives quickly. Balancing both often requires tuning generation parameters, batching strategies, and network buffers so that the stream feels smooth without sacrificing overall throughput.

Think of it this way

Think of ordering a pizza: TTFT is the moment the delivery driver knocks on your door with the first hot slice, giving you instant satisfaction, while total latency is the time until the whole pizza arrives. A quick knock makes you feel served fast, but if you have to wait forever for the rest of the pizza, the experience suffers.

In code

from fastapi import FastAPI
from fastapi.responses import StreamingResponse
import asyncio

app = FastAPI()

async def fake_generator(prompt: str):
    for i in range(5):
        await asyncio.sleep(0.2)  # simulate token generation
        yield f"token{i} "

@app.post("/stream")
async def stream(prompt: str):
    return StreamingResponse(fake_generator(prompt), media_type="text/plain")
The endpoint streams tokens as they are yielded, letting the client see the first token after ~0.2 s (TTFT) while the full response arrives after the loop finishes.

Why it matters for AI Eng interviews

Employers look for engineers who can build low‑TTFT, high‑throughput services for Retrieval‑Augmented Generation (RAG) and autonomous agents, instrument them with observability to track latency spikes, and design MCP (model‑container‑pipeline) patterns that keep latency predictable for production‑grade evals and fine‑tuning loops.
One thing to remember
Minimize TTFT to win the user's perception, but also keep total latency in check to deliver complete, reliable answers.
