Day 65: MCP Transports: Choosing Between Local and Remote Servers
TL;DR
When designing an MCP architecture, choosing the right transport method between local and remote servers is crucial for efficient data exchange and system performance.

In plain English

In the context of Model Serving and MCP (Model Control Plane), transports refer to how data is communicated between different components or servers.
Local servers are suitable for development, testing, and small-scale deployments where data is not too large, and the system can handle the load. On the other hand, remote servers are more appropriate for large-scale deployments, distributed systems, or when dealing with big data, as they can handle higher loads and provide better scalability.
The choice between local and remote servers depends on the specific requirements of the project, including factors such as data size, system load, and scalability needs.

Think of it this way

Choosing between local and remote servers can be likened to deciding whether to cook a meal in your own kitchen or order takeout from a restaurant. If you're cooking for a small group, your kitchen might be sufficient, but if you're hosting a large party, ordering from a restaurant or hiring a catering service might be more efficient and scalable.

Why it matters for AI Eng interviews

Understanding the differences between local and remote servers and when to use each is critical in AI Engineering roles, especially when designing MCP architectures. This knowledge is essential for evaluating system performance, fine-tuning models, and ensuring observability in production environments, all of which are key aspects of many AI Engineering job descriptions, including those that mention RAG (Reliability, Availability, and Governance) and agent-based systems.
One thing to remember
When deciding between local and remote servers, consider the scalability needs and system load of your project to ensure efficient data exchange and optimal system performance.
