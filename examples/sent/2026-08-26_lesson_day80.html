Day 80: Guardrails and Safety Layers
TL;DR
Learn how to filter inputs, validate outputs, and detect jailbreak attempts to keep LLM deployments trustworthy.

In plain English

Large language models (LLMs) are powerful, but they can be steered into harmful behavior by malicious prompts or unexpected output formats. Input filtering checks user requests for prohibited content, profanity, or attempts to manipulate the model’s system messages. Output validation then inspects the model’s response to ensure it complies with policy before it reaches the end user.
Jailbreak detection is a special case of input filtering that looks for patterns known to bypass safety rules, such as cleverly worded instructions or encoded payloads. By combining these layers—pre‑check, post‑check, and continuous monitoring—you create a defense‑in‑depth system that reduces risk without sacrificing model usefulness.

Think of it this way

Think of a restaurant kitchen: a chef (the model) receives orders (inputs) from diners. The host (input filter) screens orders for allergens or prohibited items, and the sous‑chef (output validator) tastes each dish before it leaves the kitchen to ensure it meets health standards. If someone tries to smuggle a banned ingredient in a disguised form, the host’s detection system (jailbreak detector) catches it before the dish is cooked.
